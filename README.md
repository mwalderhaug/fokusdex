# Fokusdex

A focus timer as a game: every minute you focus earns XP and coins, and every session lures out wild Pokémon (generations 1–5) that you can catch, evolve and collect.

- Plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript with no build step. Open `index.html`, or use GitHub Pages.
- Share it by sending the link. Each player picks a partner and up to four subjects of their own on first start. Other things (job applications, music, reading and the like) can be logged too, at half value, and their hours are kept apart from the subject hours.
- Everything is saved in the browser, so each player and device has its own game. Sync between devices is optional (Profile → Sync) and uses a private GitHub repo.
- `data.js` is built from the open data files of [PokeAPI](https://github.com/PokeAPI/pokeapi).
- Battles open with "Done for today", and you then choose one of three modes for that battle day (the other two stay closed until the next one, and one of the three pays bonus coins each day): the Trainer League (your own team against trainers scaled to it, endless, laps of 10 battles where the class rises as you go from a trainer to a Champion, the opponent kept hidden until the intro, and a small chance of Cynthia or Ghetsis in a Champions Edition as the final, each with a film of their own, XP for the whole team afterwards, Potions and Revives from the Shop usable from the Bag; one loss closes it for the day) and the Battle Frontier (open seven balls and pick four rental Pokémon, everything at Lv 50, four trainers and then a boss, win coins, items and encounters, and after a win swap one of theirs for one of yours on a screen that shows both teams and locks nothing before you press Next; two runs a day) or the World Tournament, a daily knockout cup (three rounds, three on three, your Pokémon all set to the team's level, the opponent's team shown before you pick). Battles are where most coins come from, and they pay more the closer the day's focus is to the daily goal. About every third battle day is a special event, drawn when the mode is chosen and shown as a symbol above it that opens an explanation: free Mega forms, a type or three Pokémon from the box that are stronger for the day, more coins, an extra attempt, better shiny odds or cheaper training. Events only help the player, never the opponents, and the home screen changes its look and order for the day. Legendary raids appear from studying (daily goal, week streaks): one legendary against your whole team, kept secret until you stand in front of it, with its own film, entrance and place, and the place takes part in the fight (a beaten Mewtwo can rise again as Dark Mewtwo for a second round). Catching shows what is waiting as dots by rarity, gives coins for new species, perfect throws and catches in a row, and reveals each catch's potential in stars. Coins also buy encounter packs in the Shop (every pack has a small chance of a legendary, which stays unknown until its film plays in the grass), and training for your own Pokémon on their cards (potential and training points, the game's IVs and EVs). Mega Evolution: the first Champion you beat gives the Mega Ring; a Pokémon holding a Mega Stone can then Mega Evolve once a battle, Champions do the same, and the legendaries that have a Mega or primal form change into it when they become enraged in a raid. A new player gets a seven-step start guide, a small question mark in the corner of every page opens the question hub (topics with short explanations and pictures), and the first time a Pokémon is trained the game explains potential and training points. Fair play: a battle is saved after every turn and continues where it was if the app is closed (giving up counts as a loss), a device clock that has been set back or ahead pauses sessions and battles until it is right, only a newer save can replace the one on the device, badge rewards need hours logged in the app, and a session started in the middle of the night or after a very long day asks for an honest answer first. The save itself is an ordinary file, so someone who edits it by hand can still give themselves anything. "Rules and rewards" on the home screen lists what every mode gives. A demo mode (Profile, or `#lab` in the address) lets you try any class and any raid without rewards.
- Optional private resource pack: each player can keep their own arenas, HP boxes, intro graphics, move animations, sounds and battle music in a folder called `pack` in their private sync repo. The app reads it with the sync key and stores the files on the device (`pack.js`). Nothing from such a pack is part of this repository, and without one the battles use the effects drawn with code.
- Move animations from Elite Battle: DX: if the pack holds them, battles play the EBDX move animations with their camera moves, lighting and layered effects. The app only contains a player for them: `ebx.js` is a small interpreter for the subset of Ruby the animation scripts are written in (it can only compute and move sprites) plus the building blocks EBDX gives them, and `ebxp.js` draws the result with WebGL in three layers around the two Pokémon. The scripts (as data) and the images they use live in the player's private pack, never here. Without them the battles fall back to the sheet animations or the effects drawn with code, and the Pause menu in a battle can switch back to those at any time (Moves: DX / Classic).
- Difficulty (Profile): Easy, Normal or Hard. Lower levels give weaker opponents and more items per battle; higher levels pay more coins and XP. Battle rewards also follow today's focus measured against your own daily goal, and the first win of the day pays double.
- New areas (Johto, Hoenn, Sinnoh, Unova) open by themselves at a trainer level or a number of species caught, whichever comes first.
- In battle: type tags by both HP boxes and a hint on each move (super effective, not very effective, no effect), both optional. Pokémon have a gender, can be given nicknames from their card, and are drawn larger or smaller after their Pokédex height, with a camera that pulls back for the big ones.
- Your character: pick who you are under Profile (the protagonists of the games, or any trainer or boss). The character is your profile picture and stands on your side in the intro before boss battles. The intro waits for a tap ("Press to enter") before the fight starts.
- Files: `bossf.js` (the films for the two special League finals), `help.js` (start guide and question hub), `game.js` (rules and saving), `battle.js` (battle engine), `league.js` (trainers, League, Frontier, World Tournament, raids, demo, rewards), `bfx.js` (move animations drawn with code), `ebx.js` and `ebxp.js` (player for EBDX animations), `raid.js` (the film engine for legendary raids), `raidf.js` (the films) and `raidx.js` (entrances, places and events in the raid battle, all drawn with code), `pack.js` (the private pack), `bui.js` (battle screens), `ui.js` (everything else on screen).

Credits for the animation system the player is written for: [Elite Battle: DX](https://luka-sj.com/res/ebdx) by Luka S.J. and its contributors, and the EBDX animation pack by NikDie. None of their code or graphics is in this repository.
