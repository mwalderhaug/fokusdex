# Fokusdex

A focus timer as a game: every minute you focus earns XP and coins, and every session lures out wild Pokémon (generations 1–5) that you can catch, evolve and collect.

- Plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript with no build step. Open `index.html`, or use GitHub Pages.
- Share it by sending the link. Each player picks a partner and up to four subjects of their own on first start. Other things (job applications, music, reading and the like) can be logged too, at half value, and their hours are kept apart from the subject hours.
- Everything is saved in the browser, so each player and device has its own game. Sync between devices is optional (Profile → Sync) and uses a private GitHub repo.
- `data.js` is built from the open data files of [PokeAPI](https://github.com/PokeAPI/pokeapi).
- Battles open with "Done for today" once the day has 25 minutes of focus, and you then choose one of three modes for that battle day (the other two stay closed until the next one, and one of the three pays bonus coins each day): the Trainer League (your own team against trainers scaled to it, endless, laps of 8 battles where the class rises as you go from a trainer to a Champion, the opponent kept hidden until the intro, and a small chance of Cynthia or Ghetsis in a Champions Edition as the final, each with a film of their own, XP for the whole team afterwards, Potions and Revives from the Shop usable from the Bag; one loss ends the lap and the next one starts from battle 1, a lap you win is the lap for that day, and Champions keep their ace and signature Pokémon but change the rest of the team a little from time to time) and the Battle Frontier (open seven balls and pick four rental Pokémon, everything at Lv 50, four trainers and then a boss, win coins, items, encounters and XP for your own team, and after a win swap one of theirs for one of yours on a screen that shows both teams and locks nothing before you press Next; clear a round and you keep one of your four rentals; two runs a day) or the World Tournament, a daily knockout cup (three rounds, three on three, your Pokémon all set to the team's level, the opponent's team shown before you pick, coins and XP for every round). Battles are where most coins come from, and they pay more the closer the day's focus is to the daily goal. A day is never lost: "Not today" closes it without battles, "Simulate" lets the game play the day's battles with your team for 40 % of the coins and XP and nothing else (shown before you accept, and it can be undone once), and battles left until the next day stay open, marked as yesterday's, until a new session starts. About every third battle day is a special event, drawn when the mode is chosen and shown as a symbol above it that opens an explanation: three free Mega forms that you choose yourself, a type or three Pokémon from the box that are stronger for the day, more coins, an extra attempt, better shiny odds or cheaper training. Events only help the player, never the opponents, and the home screen changes its look and order for the day. Legendary raids are rare and never on a fixed day (about once a week on Normal if you study every day, nearly twice as often on Hard and less often on Easy). One that has appeared stays hidden until the day's battles are played, simulated or skipped, and then shows itself as a surprise: one legendary against your whole team, kept secret until you stand in front of it, two attempts, with its own film, entrance and place, and the place takes part in the fight (a beaten Mewtwo is yours, and its shadow always rises as Dark Mewtwo for a second round; win that and the dark one takes your Mewtwo's place with perfect IVs, a Timid nature and full training; that fight does not stay the same from start to end; a warning comes first, and its first battle song starts with the transformation and runs on into the fight; with Arceus on your team, Dark Mewtwo cannot restore its strength when it changes). Beat it and it is yours: winning is the catch. New legendaries come first, and one you own almost never returns. Which ones show up follows your collection: mostly the common ones at first, and the biggest more often the more you have caught, Mewtwo and Arceus too. The very first raid is Mewtwo or Arceus, far too strong for a new team, so you know what is out there. Arceus and Dark Mewtwo are the hardest, with Rayquaza, Giratina and Mewtwo next: Arceus changes plate and type every third turn to resist what you last hit it with, and Deoxys shifts between its Attack and Defense Formes. A raid boss shrugs off one-hit knockout moves, and poison, burns and the like take their share of an ordinary amount of HP, not of its enormous bar. The Team screen has a Duplicates tab that lists every species you have more than one of, to sell one by one or all at once, with a mark on the ones born with top IVs. Catching shows how many encounters are waiting as plain dots (nothing about them is revealed before you meet them), gives coins for new species, perfect throws and catches in a row, and reveals each catch's potential in stars. Coins also buy what the hunt needs as the trainer level rises (Dusk Balls that hold legendaries better, one Master Ball at a time, Golden Razz Berries, Lures for more or rarer encounters, incense that multiplies the shiny odds for one to three days, a Prism Lure for a shiny raid, and Sacred Ash that brings a whole team back once in a raid), encounter packs (every pack has a small chance of a legendary, which stays unknown until its film plays in the grass), and training for your own Pokémon on their cards (EVs with the caps from the games, 252 in a stat and 510 in all, and Hyper Training from Lv 50 that makes an IV count as 31). A shiny Pokémon is born with perfect IVs and has 5 % higher stats than an ordinary one, and each card shows the species' base stats as coloured bars. Mega Evolution: the first Champion you beat gives the Mega Ring; a Pokémon holding a Mega Stone can then Mega Evolve once a battle, Champions do the same, and the legendaries that have a Mega or primal form change into it when they become enraged in a raid. A Champion or a Supreme Boss now and then leaves a rare prize on top of the rest: a purse that makes your next battle day pay 50 % more coins, a bag of coins, a lasting item to hold, a Prism Lure, a Master Ball, or a shiny waiting in the grass. Items to hold: every Pokémon can hold one thing in battle. Berries are used up (your team finds one now and then after a long session, and the Shop sells them), and lasting items such as Leftovers, Life Orb, Eviolite and the seventeen plates stay. A plate also gives Arceus its type and colour, and a raid win gives the plate of the legendary's type. A guide of ten short steps is shown to new players, and once to everyone after an update that changes the rules; it can be opened again from Profile and from the question hub (the small question mark in the corner of every page, with short explanations and pictures). The first time a Pokémon is trained the game explains IVs and EVs. Profile also has "Show the game": a trailer and an overview made for showing a friend. The trailer is short scenes on one track, a clip from a real battle (a hit, a Mega Evolution and a knockout, timed to the music on any device), and at the end the film of one legendary before the title. Apart from that one film, they show how the game goes and how much is in it without giving away what is waiting (bosses and legendaries appear only as shadows). There is also a battle with a strong rental team that plays itself or that you play, with Exit always at the top. Fair play: a battle is saved after every turn and continues where it was if the app is closed (giving up counts as a loss), a device clock that has been set back or ahead pauses sessions and battles until it is right, only a newer save can replace the one on the device, a finished focus session always comes along when two saves meet (two devices, or two windows of the app in one browser), whichever of them wins, and a window that has been in the background takes over what the other one saved before it does anything itself, badge rewards need hours logged in the app, and a session started in the middle of the night or after a very long day asks for an honest answer first. The save itself is an ordinary file, so someone who edits it by hand can still give themselves anything. "Rules and rewards" on the home screen lists what every mode gives. A sandbox lets the owner test anything without rewards: single battles against a chosen trainer, any raid, the films, free packs with the real odds, and the whole game on a copy of the save (all three modes open, a League final against a chosen opponent, a Frontier run from a chosen round, a chosen cup, and tools for coins, levels, items, raids and encounters), where nothing is stored or synced. It is an admin tool: it only shows on devices whose Sync is connected to a repository of the account named in `ADMIN` in `game.js` (change it if you run your own copy).
- Optional private resource pack: each player can keep their own arenas, HP boxes, intro graphics, move animations, sounds (for moves and for the interface: taps, menus, catches, level-ups) and battle music in a folder called `pack` in their private sync repo. The app reads it with the sync key and stores the files on the device (`pack.js`). Nothing from such a pack is part of this repository, and without one the battles use the effects drawn with code.
- Move animations from Elite Battle: DX: if the pack holds them, battles play the EBDX move animations with their camera moves, lighting and layered effects. The app only contains a player for them: `ebx.js` is a small interpreter for the subset of Ruby the animation scripts are written in (it can only compute and move sprites) plus the building blocks EBDX gives them, and `ebxp.js` draws the result with WebGL in three layers around the two Pokémon. The scripts (as data) and the images they use live in the player's private pack, never here. Without them the battles fall back to the sheet animations or the effects drawn with code, and the Pause menu in a battle can switch back to those at any time (Moves: DX / Classic).
- Music and sound: without a resource pack the app plays its own battle music, seven original tunes (one for each kind of opponent) played by a small four-voice synth in `music.js`: no sound files. With a pack, each track loops on loop points stored in the pack's manifest (`l: [start, end]` in seconds and `x`, the length of the crossfade), found by looking for the place where the recording repeats itself, so the intro plays once and the loop has no audible seam. Tracks without loop points play again from the start with the ending faded into the beginning. Music fades out after a battle instead of stopping, and in FAST speed the battle sounds are shortened with the animations.
- Difficulty (Profile): Easy, Normal or Hard. Lower levels give weaker opponents and more items per battle; higher levels pay more. Hard pays the most of everything: coins, XP, Rare Candy, and legendary raids come nearly twice as often. Battle rewards also follow today's focus measured against your own daily goal, and the first win of the day pays double.
- New areas (Johto, Hoenn, Sinnoh, Unova) open by themselves at a trainer level or a number of species caught, whichever comes first.
- In battle: type tags by both HP boxes and a hint on each move (super effective, not very effective, no effect), both optional. Pokémon have a gender, can be given nicknames from their card, and are drawn larger or smaller after their Pokédex height, with a camera that pulls back for the big ones.
- Your character: pick who you are under Profile (the protagonists of the games, or any trainer or boss). The character is your profile picture and stands on your side in the intro before boss battles. The intro waits for a tap ("Press to enter") before the fight starts.
- Achievements and rank: a tap on your picture opens your achievements (study hours, day streak, daily goals, species caught overall and for each of the 18 types, shinies, legendaries, evolutions, League wins and laps, Champions beaten, the two special League finals, Frontier streak, trophies, raids and more). Each has up to five steps. Every step you claim pays a reward and counts as one medal (132 in all), and medals raise your rank from Rookie through Novice, Skilled, Expert and Master to Elite. The rank is the frame and stamp around your picture.
- Files: `bossf.js` (the films for the two special League finals and the place the battle against Ghetsis stands on), `darkf.js` (Darkrai's film and place, and what happens to Dark Mewtwo in the middle of its fight), `help.js` (guide and question hub), `show.js` (the raid arrival and capture, the sandbox and "Show the game"), `music.js` (the built-in battle music), `game.js` (rules and saving), `battle.js` (battle engine), `league.js` (trainers, League, Frontier, World Tournament, raids, the simulated battle day, rewards), `bfx.js` (move animations drawn with code), `ebx.js` and `ebxp.js` (player for EBDX animations), `raid.js` (the film engine for legendary raids), `raidf.js` (the films) and `raidx.js` (entrances, places and events in the raid battle, all drawn with code), `pack.js` (the private pack), `bui.js` (battle screens), `ui.js` (everything else on screen).
