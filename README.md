# Fokusdex

A focus timer as a game: every minute you focus earns XP and coins, and every session lures out wild Pokémon (generations 1–5) that you can catch, evolve and collect.

- Plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript with no build step. Open `index.html`, or use GitHub Pages.
- Share it by sending the link. Each player picks a partner and up to four subjects of their own on first start. Other things (job applications, music, reading and the like) can be logged too, at half value, and their hours are kept apart from the subject hours.
- Everything is saved in the browser, so each player and device has its own game. Sync between devices is optional (Profile → Sync) and uses a private GitHub repo.
- `data.js` is built from the open data files of [PokeAPI](https://github.com/PokeAPI/pokeapi).
- Battles open with "Done for today": the Trainer League (your own team against trainers scaled to it, endless, laps of 12 battles with a trainer and then a boss through nine classes from Gym Leader to Supreme Boss, XP for the whole team afterwards, Potions and Revives from the Shop usable from the Bag; one loss closes it for the day) and the Battle Frontier (pick 4 of 7 rental Pokémon, everything at Lv 50, six trainers and then a boss, win coins, items and encounters; two runs a day). Each day one of the two has a bonus. Battles are where most coins come from, and they pay more the closer the day's focus is to the daily goal. The World Tournament is a daily knockout cup (three rounds, three on three, your Pokémon all set to the team's level, the opponent's team shown before you pick). About every third battle day is a special event, revealed when the battles open: free Mega forms, a type or three Pokémon from the box that are stronger for the day, more coins, an extra attempt, better shiny odds or cheaper training. Events only help, and the home screen changes its look and order for the day. Legendary raids appear from studying (daily goal, week streaks): one legendary against your whole team, with its own film, entrance and place, and the place takes part in the fight (a beaten Mewtwo can rise again as Dark Mewtwo for a second round). Coins also buy encounter packs in the Shop, and training for your own Pokémon on their cards (potential and training points, the game's IVs and EVs). Mega Evolution: the first Champion you beat gives the Mega Ring; a Pokémon holding a Mega Stone can then Mega Evolve once a battle, Champions do the same, and the legendaries that have a Mega or primal form change into it when they become enraged in a raid. "Rules and rewards" on the home screen lists what every mode gives. A demo mode (Profile, or `#lab` in the address) lets you try any class and any raid without rewards.
- Optional private resource pack: each player can keep their own arenas, HP boxes, intro graphics, move animations, sounds and battle music in a folder called `pack` in their private sync repo. The app reads it with the sync key and stores the files on the device (`pack.js`). Nothing from such a pack is part of this repository, and without one the battles use the effects drawn with code.
- Move animations from Elite Battle: DX: if the pack holds them, battles play the EBDX move animations with their camera moves, lighting and layered effects. The app only contains a player for them: `ebx.js` is a small interpreter for the subset of Ruby the animation scripts are written in (it can only compute and move sprites) plus the building blocks EBDX gives them, and `ebxp.js` draws the result with WebGL in three layers around the two Pokémon. The scripts (as data) and the images they use live in the player's private pack, never here. Without them the battles fall back to the sheet animations or the effects drawn with code, and the Pause menu in a battle can switch back to those at any time (Moves: DX / Classic).
- Difficulty (Profile): Easy, Normal or Hard. Lower levels give weaker opponents and more items per battle; higher levels pay more coins and XP. Battle rewards also follow today's focus measured against your own daily goal, and the first win of the day pays double.
- New areas (Johto, Hoenn, Sinnoh, Unova) open by themselves at a trainer level or a number of species caught, whichever comes first.
- In battle: type tags by both HP boxes and a hint on each move (super effective, not very effective, no effect), both optional. Pokémon have a gender, can be given nicknames from their card, and are drawn larger or smaller after their Pokédex height, with a camera that pulls back for the big ones.
- Your character: pick who you are under Profile (the protagonists of the games, or any trainer or boss). The character is your profile picture and stands on your side in the intro before boss battles. The intro waits for a tap ("Press to enter") before the fight starts.
- Files: `game.js` (rules and saving), `battle.js` (battle engine), `league.js` (trainers, League, Frontier, World Tournament, raids, demo, rewards), `bfx.js` (move animations drawn with code), `ebx.js` and `ebxp.js` (player for EBDX animations), `raid.js` (the film engine for legendary raids), `raidf.js` (the films) and `raidx.js` (entrances, places and events in the raid battle, all drawn with code), `pack.js` (the private pack), `bui.js` (battle screens), `ui.js` (everything else on screen).

Credits for the animation system the player is written for: [Elite Battle: DX](https://luka-sj.com/res/ebdx) by Luka S.J. and its contributors, and the EBDX animation pack by NikDie. None of their code or graphics is in this repository.
